# 5. Plain statements, with ambiguity shown instead of hedged

Date: 2026-10-02. Status: Accepted.

Redline states what a cited sentence says in plain, direct language, with no hedging words such as "may", "might" or "could potentially". When a clause can genuinely be read two ways, Redline says so and shows both readings. An ambiguous renewal-and-exit clause goes in **Negotiate before signing**, because the ambiguity is itself something to fix before signing.

Hedged language is safe but tells the buyer nothing, while unqualified confidence is useful but sometimes wrong. Redline can speak plainly because every claim sits next to its citation, so the buyer can check the reading against the text.

## Consequences
- Redline gives up the legal safety that hedged wording provides.
- When Redline reads a clause wrongly, it will say so confidently. The protection against that is the citation next to the claim, not the wording.
- Prompts and tests must catch hedging words as a defect, and must check that ambiguous clauses show both readings.
