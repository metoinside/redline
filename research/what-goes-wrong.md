# What goes wrong: clause types that burn people

## Method note
- Web searches used: 9 of 12. Pages read (WebFetch attempts): 4 of 15, of which 2 failed or were unreadable (CT DOB site unreachable; NACA PDF returned binary, so only the search-snippet figure is used). Search-result snippets supplied several figures; those are flagged "snippet only".
- Stopped after 9 sourced findings (cap was 8).
- The ranking is NOT a counted-mentions ranking. It is a judgment ordering built from cited statistics that measure different things (complaints per day, % of survey respondents, complaint counts in different states and years). The sources are not comparable on a common denominator. Treat the order as directional.
- Not a source of consumer complaints: no forum scraping (Reddit, etc.) was done.

## Ranking caveats
- Ranks 1 to 3 rest on real statistics but different units and populations (survey % vs complaint counts vs one state), so the order between them is not defensible. Payment ranks first because the prevalence is highest (62% to 91% of freelancers), not because complaints were counted against other clauses.
- Complaint volume is not clause-level. FTC and AG data count a complaint category, not which contract clause caused it. Nonpayment is partly a behavior problem, not a clause a reader can catch in advance; a reader tool would only help where the contract's payment terms were weak or absent.
- Arbitration appears rare in complaint data (0.04%) but is likely under-reported: people rarely know the clause exists until a dispute.
- Non-compete figures measure coverage, not harm.
- Many figures are from snippets, not read in full.
- Selection bias: freelancer surveys come from an advocacy group promoting the Freelance Isn't Free Act.
