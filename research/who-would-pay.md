# Redline research: who would pay

## Method
- Web searches used: 10 of 12. Pages read (WebFetch): 3 of 15.
- Several findings come from search-result summaries, not full-page reads. Those are marked "(snippet)".
- Many sources are vendors or law firms with a commercial interest (Rocket Lawyer, ContractsCounsel, Smallpdf, Kolmogorov Law). Treat them as directional.

## Segments ranked by sharpness of pain (evidence-based, not proven)

1. Freelancers / independent contractors (large, high pain, but spend is low)
   - Size: 28% of US knowledge workers (more than 20M) freelance, earning $1.5T in 2024 (Upwork Future Workforce Index 2025, snippet). https://smallbiztrends.com/upwork-future-workforce-index-2025-freelancer-growth/
   - Older, broader Upwork count: 64M Americans freelanced in 2023, 38% of the workforce. https://investors.upwork.com/news-releases/news-release-details/upwork-study-finds-64-million-americans-freelanced-2023-adding
   - Pain: Freelancers Union 2014 survey. 71% had trouble collecting payment at some point. The average loss was $5,968 (13% of annual income). Only about 25% consistently have written contracts. Contracts correlated with 13.7% higher income, but 38.8% of contract users still had payment trouble. Under 1% used the legal system to recover money (snippet). https://onlabor.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/05/FU_NonpaymentReport_r3.pdf (data is from 2014, so it is old)
2. Small business owners (clearest "skip review because of cost/time" evidence)
   - Rocket Lawyer 2026 SMB survey (Researchscape): nearly 1 in 4 owners sign contracts exactly as presented. Time pressure is cited as a main reason, along with low confidence in legal language. Sample size and cost figures were not published on the page. https://www.rocketlawyer.com/the-briefing-room/1-in-4-business-owners-sign-contracts-as-is
   - A secondary summary says respondents cite high cost as the reason for not using an attorney (snippet; the figure of "three million small businesses" is unclear). https://researchscape.com/inthenews/1-in-4-smbs-sign-contracts-as-is-is-your-speed-costing-you
3. Creators / influencers (high-value deals, lawyers priced at about $670-$820)
   - Lawyer bids on ContractsCounsel for influencer agreements average $670 for review and $980 for drafting (134 bids; blended $820). Mid-to-high five-figure deals run "low-to-mid thousands" for full review and negotiation (a law firm's claim). https://www.contractscounsel.com/m/influencer-agreement-pricing and https://heitnerlegal.com/2026/05/18/why-influencers-need-a-lawyer-to-review-and-negotiate-brand-deal-contracts/
   - No survey found on how many creators sign without review.
4. Tenants (widespread non-reading, but the evidence is UK, not US)
   - UK surveys: only 24% of tenants read the tenancy agreement before signing, and 39% admit to signing contracts they don't understand (Weightmans). 73% weren't aware of what they agreed to (Hillarys, 2,100 tenants). https://thenegotiator.co.uk/news/rental-market/shock-most-people-dont-read-tenancy-agreements/ (snippet)
   - This shows pain and low reading, not willingness to pay.
5. Job-seekers (employment/non-compete) and musicians: no usable evidence found beyond lawyer price points (see below).

## Willingness-to-pay evidence

Current spend on lawyers (the "alternative" price)
- Average US lawyer hourly rate is $349 in 2025 (Clio). Corporate averages $461. Law firm blended rate averages $311. https://clio.com/resources/legal-trends/compare-lawyer-rates/
- ContractsCounsel: average contract-review project $608. Average business-lawyer review fee $485. Flat fees typically $250-$750. Hourly $150-$300. https://www.contractscounsel.com/b/contract-review-cost
- ContractsCounsel flat-fee averages by type:
  - NDA: $204
  - Consulting agreement: $437
  - Business contract: $466
  - Executive employment contract review: $540
  - Business partnership: $538
  - Business purchase: $900
  - Source: the same search-result summary of https://www.contractscounsel.com/b/contract-review-cost and related pages (snippet)

Existing products and price anchors (what the market already charges for AI-style review)
- Rocket Lawyer already bundles "unlimited contract reviews" (upload, key terms, red flags) in all tiers: Standard $34.99/mo (about $12.41/mo billed yearly), Plus $49.99/mo, Pro $64.99/mo (about $29.08/mo yearly). Attorney Q&A is limited to 12 questions on Standard and unlimited on Pro. https://www.rocketlawyer.com/pricing
- LegalZoom legal plans are listed at $39-$49/mo (third-party aggregator, snippet; not verified against LegalZoom directly). https://subger.com/en/service/legalzoom

Thresholds for "too expensive"
- No explicit dollar threshold found. The only stated evidence is qualitative: SMB owners skip counsel because of high cost and time (see Rocket Lawyer links above). A lawyer at $250-$750 per contract, or $349/hr, is evidently above what many will pay for routine contracts.
- B2B data point: 86% of legal departments would pay 11-20% above standard rates for AI-fluent contract attorneys (Paragon Legal, via snippet). This is about attorney premiums, not consumer WTP. https://www.abajournal.com/news/article/ai-may-push-more-contract-attorneys-to-cut-pay-new-study-shows

Evidence against paying
- 56% of AI users say they seek legal advice from ChatGPT or other AI (Kolmogorov Law survey, n=1,000; a law firm survey syndicated by Stacker). A free substitute is already in use. https://stacker.com/stories/technology/chatgpt-subpoena-revolution-when-your-ai-conversations-become-court-evidence (snippet)
- 1 in 5 professionals would choose an AI-written contract over a lawyer-reviewed one for a $100k+ deal (Smallpdf research, snippet; could not verify methodology because the page was truncated). https://smallpdf.com/blog/ai-contracts-in-small-business
- Under 1% of freelancers used the legal system to recover lost pay (Freelancers Union). Legal spend is rare, so people may not pay even when the pain is acute.
- Rocket Lawyer already gives unlimited AI contract review inside a $35-65/month membership, so a standalone tool competes with a bundled feature.
- Free ready-made contracts are widely adopted by small business owners without a lawyer (snippet from the Rocket Lawyer and Researchscape results above).

## Gaps (could not find)
- Any direct stated willingness-to-pay figure for AI contract review from consumers, freelancers or small business owners.
- Segment-specific data on how often each segment actually pays a lawyer for contract review (frequency and annual spend). Clio's consumer-side spend data was not retrieved.
- US tenant data (the surveys found are UK) and any data on non-compete or employment-agreement review demand.
- Creator and musician surveys on signing without review.
- Churn, conversion or pricing data for consumer AI legal tools beyond Rocket Lawyer's list prices.
- Fresh freelancer non-payment data (the Freelancers Union study is from 2014).
- The full text of the Smallpdf and Researchscape surveys; their sample sizes and methods are unverified.
